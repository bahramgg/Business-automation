## The problem

Orders came in through three separate routes: phone, Instagram DMs, and
WhatsApp. During the rush there was no time to reply, and messages sent after
closing were effectively lost. The menu was maintained in three places and the
prices disagreed. Nobody had a complete picture of a night's orders, and
reporting meant counting paper tickets by hand.

This wasn't a sales problem. It was an absence of process, and it touched all
four domains.

## What was built

**Acquisition.** A lightweight site with an always-current menu, so a new
customer sees prices without asking and the route to an order is obvious.

**Sales & response.** Automated response on Telegram and a site widget, reading
the menu and prices from that single source, handling the recurring questions,
and recording orders with their details.

**Operations.** Every order carries an explicit status — received, preparing,
sent — and the kitchen and the till both see the same view.

**Data & reporting.** Orders land in the customer database at the same time, and
weekly sales and top items are compiled automatically.

## Result

Busy hours no longer drop messages, and late-night questions aren't unanswered
by morning. The menu has one source of truth and a price change happens once.
For the first time, management can see the week without counting tickets.
